          PinsWhere the scanners found something: a marker over the building, in the color of the worst of them, taller the more there are. Point at one for the tally, click it to read them - a collapsed directory opens what is below it too
          + beside a findingPuts it in the backpack without walking out to its bug. It is the same catch: the bug stops walking, and it stays there until the scanners stop reporting it
          BThe backpack: every bug caught, in either view, still open or fixed since. Click one to go back to where it was caught
          The figureWhere you were standing in walk mode, facing the way you were facing. V puts you back there

          Walk mode (V)

          MouseLook around: the pointer is captured at the reticle (Esc frees it, a click on the map captures it again; or drag to look)
          W A S D / arrowsMove / turn
          ShiftRun
          SpaceJump (flying: straight up)
          FFly on / off. Flying, W / S move where you look (look down and press W to dive); Space / C go straight up / down
          ClickUse what is in your hands - a fishing rod, a net, a camera, a bubble wand, a tracking dart: the module it reaches is tagged, selected and its dependencies light up. Using it again on a tagged building shows its details
          BugsEvery finding a scanner reported keeps to a lap on the building it belongs to, colored by severity - around it on the street, along its facade at some height, across its roof, or flying a wider circuit round it, each beetle standing out of whatever it is holding on to, or leaning into the corners if it is holding on to nothing. Use any tool on one to catch it and read what it was carrying; the HUD counts what is left, and what you catch goes in the backpack (B on the map) until the source is fixed. The map view collects the same findings from the panel, if you would rather not walk
          TrackerThe sweep in the corner turns with you: a dot per bug still out there, a ring per module already tagged, an arrow on the rim for anything beyond its range
          1 … 7Take out the tool in that slot; T walks along the row. Each is good for its own quarry - the rod, the tracking dart and the nail gun tag buildings, the butterfly net and the bubble wand catch bugs, the camera does either - and the reticle dims when what you are pointing at is not for the tool in hand. Each also has a range, and they differ: the wand barely crosses a street, the nail gun carries a block or so, the dart further, the rod and the grapple furthest. Past it the reticle dims too and the shot falls short. The net throws nothing at all: it has to be swung within arm's length, and the camera alone photographs at any distance. The camera's back screen shows what its lens is on
          Grapple gunThe one tool that does nothing to what it hits. Hook a building and the line pulls you up the facade and onto its roof; from up there, aim over the edge at anything lower and the same shot is the way down. Space cuts the line
          HPut the tool away, or take it out again. Only the drawing of it: it still works, and what it throws then leaves from your eye
          Hold right buttonLook through the scope (zoom)
          EnterShow the details of what the reticle is on (dependencies, source) - the same as a second dart. The mouse is freed for reading; click the map to walk on. O opens the file in your editor.
          WheelZoom in / out
          + −Planet size (curvature); [ ] do the same
          V / MBack to the map (Esc frees the mouse first, then leaves). Coming back puts you where you were standing, unless you picked something on the map in the meantime
