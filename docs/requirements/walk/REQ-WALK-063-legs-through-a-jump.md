## Statement

The walker's legs (REQ-WALK-059) **shall** move through a jump as a body's do,
by how fast the walker is going up or down, rather than holding one pose in the
air:

- leaving the ground, the legs **shall** push off straight, the toes pointed,
  one leg trailing, and the arms swing up with them;
- rising, the knees **shall** come up, tucked at the top of the jump;
- coming down - from a jump, or off an edge - the legs **shall** reach down for
  the ground under the hips, the knees a little bent, the arms out for balance;
- going off the ground and back onto it **shall** be eased, so that stepping
  off a curb is not a jump.

Landing, the knees **shall** give and straighten again: at once, deeper and
for longer the harder the feet came down - not at all for a step off a curb, a
little for a jump, most of the way down for a drop off a roof - each foot
staying flat under the hips while the hips come down, the body leaning a little
over its knees and the arms coming forward against it. No joint **shall** jump
between frames, and the hips **shall** go down no faster than the walker was
falling. A jump **shall** still leave the ground at once: none of this delays
it. Sat on a ride, none of it applies.

## Rationale

A body that snaps into one tucked pose the moment the feet leave the ground, and
out of it the moment they are back, reads as a puppet. The push, the tuck, the
reach and the give are what make a jump look like it had weight - and, seen from
behind, how hard a landing was.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Seen from behind, a jump leaves the ground with the legs straight and the
   toes pointed, tucks the knees at the top, and reaches down with the legs
   coming down.
2. Landing a jump, the knees bend and the hips dip, and the walker is standing
   straight again within half a second; a drop off a roof bends them further and
   for longer; a step off a curb does not visibly bend them.
3. Through the landing, the feet stay flat on the ground under the hips.
4. Jumping takes off on the same frame as before.
